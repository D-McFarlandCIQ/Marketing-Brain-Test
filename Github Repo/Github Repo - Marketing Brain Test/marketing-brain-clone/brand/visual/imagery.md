# Photography, charts, and iconography

## Photography (pp. 123–129)

Show our audience as **confident, intelligent heroes**, empowered by the platform.
Show people working on their own, in person and remotely, and in natural
collaboration.

**Sources (for now):** Adobe Stock, Unsplash, Pexels.

**Subjects:**

- **Hero testimonials and personas:** candid portraits of commission specialists and
  sales team members.
- **Situational:** collaboration in person and remotely, and people working solo on
  tablets and desktops. Show the product in context where possible.
- **Target demographic:** ages 20–45, showing a range of diverse people.

| Look for | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Tidy, natural workspaces that are neat but not immaculate | Messy desks, clutter, backgrounds full of knick-knacks |
| Displays and devices, ideally with CIQ product inserted on screen | Mobile as the focal point. A phone nearby is fine. |
| Soft depth of field, in the foreground or background, and plenty of natural light | Everything in sharp focus, closed-in spaces, staged or over-bright lighting |
| Natural gestures and expressive hands. Smiles are fine, but not everyone grinning. | Forced gestures, ear-to-ear grins, head-back laughing, no eye contact, awkward handshakes |

Subjects can look at the camera or just off to the side.

**Cropping and use:**

- Crop for focus and simplicity, especially when combining photos with graphics or
  vignettes.
- **Photography is a hero moment. Don't overuse it.** Combine it with the
  "Driving Force" shapes ([graphic-language.md](graphic-language.md)), color fields,
  product abstractions, or other photos to tell a story.

## Charts and graphs (pp. 130–135)

Charts appear mostly in B2B communications.

- **Show one or two core ideas per chart.** Keep it simple and highlight only what
  matters.
- Use **bold graphics, expressive color, and a fine type hierarchy in Fakt**. Pick out
  key figures with contrasting weights.
- Give fills a **subtle gradient** within each color for depth. Flat tones are fine
  when a chart has many series and clarity comes first.
- Use **2–3 colors** for simple charts, or a spectrum across the palette for more
  complex ones. Both light and dark backgrounds work.
- **Bring the brand team in** on any work that's heavy with charts.

## Iconography (pp. 136–141)

Icons are visual shorthand. **They shouldn't carry important information on their
own**, so always pair them with text.

| System | Where to use it | Style |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brand icons** | All print and digital work outside the core product | A custom three-color set in **Galaxy, Hedge, and CIQ White**. Angular plus rounded, straightforward, grounded. |
| **Product (Quotient) icons** | Inside the product and in product vignettes | **Font Awesome**. Simple, light, linear. Usually Galaxy, Hedge, or White. New icons should match Font Awesome's style. |

Get the brand icon set from the brand team or the Notion Brand Portal
([resources.md](../resources.md)).
