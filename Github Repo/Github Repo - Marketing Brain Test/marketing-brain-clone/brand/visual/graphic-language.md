# Graphic language

## The 20° "Driving Force" angle (pp. 104–110)

Everything in the graphic system comes from one detail of the Wordmark. **The tail of
the "Q" and the angles of the "v" are both 20°.** Extended out of the Wordmark, that
diagonal becomes the **"Driving Force"** (or **"Business Driver"**) angle. It stands
for CaptivateIQ as a strategic business driver, "transforming your largest sales
expense into a business driver."

The angle is paired with **right angles and rounded elements**. These add
approachability and echo the round letterforms of the Wordmark.

### The four base forms (p. 107)

The building blocks of the system, which can be recombined freely:

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Flexibility** | Feels like a table |
| **Growth** | Echoes the "growth graph" from the previous identity |
| **Modeling** | Hints at configuring and connecting |
| **Momentum** | Forward movement, being a business driver |

### Using it

- Rotate, stretch, combine, layer, overlap, and frame the forms. The manual encourages
  open-ended exploration within the system.
- Use the forms to **frame real customers and sales professionals** in authentic,
  confident compositions.
- **Evolution (pp. 111–115):** combine the 20° angle with a **rectangular grid**, which
  suggests a spreadsheet, and with chart shapes (bar, pie, line) to make playful
  patterns about modeling and strategy.
- Other contrasting Wordmark forms can be pulled out too, to hint at "wonder" and
  "intelligence".

## Product vignettes (pp. 116–122)

Focused product screenshots that show how CIQ makes commission management automated,
motivating, and captivating, for audiences from RevOps to comp analysts to individual
sellers.

**Anatomy:**

- **Tell a short story.** Show one screen or a focused feature set. **Never show the
  whole screen or every feature.** Vignettes placed side by side should build into an
  end-to-end story.
- **Use the 8-point rounding scale.** Corner radii are **4, 8, 16, or 24px**. 8 is the
  "CIQ common number", and multiples of 4 are fine. The rounding echoes the Wordmark.
- **Build at 100% scale** so corners stay consistent, then scale up or down **in 25%
  steps**.
- Keep **type sizes, colors, table styles, and buttons consistent.** They should hint
  at the Quotient product design system.
- Add **subtle drop shadows** on highlighted features for depth.
- Full-screen views are allowed when the goal is to show the whole suite, and they can
  be abstract or detailed.
- **Data must feel real but stay anonymous and fictional.** Never use real customer
  data.
- Pair vignettes with photography, color, and the graphic pattern, and leave generous
  white space.

Ask the brand team for **finished vignettes or starter files.** Don't build your own
from raw screenshots.

## Spot illustrations (pp. 142–145)

Use spot illustrations primarily in customer-facing product moments, to create
surprise, delight, levity, or even vulnerability.

- **Every illustration is built on the 20° angle.**
- **"Immersive isometric" style:** rotating the 20° diamond by 90° gives an isometric
  grid, and illustrations are drawn on that grid.
- Make them **conceptual**: short narratives about ICM and SPM ideas.
- Use them **sparingly**, in high-touch product moments and premium collateral (data
  visualizations, collateral covers, key digital events).
- **Make new ones with the brand team.**

## Calcy (p. 185)

Calcy is the "official / unofficial" CIQ mascot, "a self-proclaimed math and modeling
nerd". It's reserved for customer and internal events and very limited prospect
engagement. **It's always a side note or accent, never a substitute for the primary
brand.**
